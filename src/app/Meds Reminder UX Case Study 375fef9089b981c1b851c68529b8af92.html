Design Process : PXD (Product eXperience Design)

PXD (Product eXperience Design) is a structured approach focused on designing seamless, effective, and empathetic healthcare experiences for patients, caregivers, and medical professionals.

“PXD” vs “Design Thinking”

✅ Healthcare Focused – PXD aligns with medical needs, compliance, & patient safety.

✅ Patient-Centric – Prioritizes empathy and usability for patients & caregivers.

✅ Regulatory Compliance – Adheres to healthcare laws(HIPPA/ GDPR) & ethical considerations.

✅ Clinical Validation – Integrates medical research and expert insights.

PXD aligns with “SAFe agile”

PXD is user-centered & design-driven, while 🚀 SAFe scales Agile across organizations.🎯 Both focus on customer needs, collaboration 🤝, and iterative improvements 🔄.📌 PXD integrates with SAFe’s PI Planning, Agile Release Trains (ARTs), and backlog management.

Key Questions asked

To better understand users' daily medication habits, we conducted interviews with older adults and caregivers. These conversations revealed common challenges, unmet needs, and opportunities to improve medication adherence.

Key Findings

  • ✅ Users forget medications due to busy schedules or memory issues.
  • 📢 Simple, clear reminders are preferred over complex notifications.
  • 🔎 Large fonts & high contrast improve usability for older adults.
  • ⏰ Customizable reminder frequency & timing are essential.
  • 👥 Caregivers benefit from shared reminder access.
  • 💊 Auto-refill reminders support prescription adherence
  • ⚡ Users prefer a quick, seamless setup.

✔ Regular Intervals:

Problem Statement 🤔

Medication non-adherence is a major healthcare challenge, causing worsening health conditions, increased hospitalizations, and higher medical costs. Many patients, especially the elderly and busy professionals , forget to take medications due to cognitive decline, hectic schedules, or lack of reminders. “

"How Might We" (HMW) framework

Breaking Down the Problem Statement:

1️⃣ How might we make it simple?

  • Design an intuitive UI with minimal steps to add and track medications.
  • Use clear visual cues and interactions for accessibility.

2️⃣ How might we ensure adherence?

  • Smart reminders with flexible snooze options.
  • Gamification & rewards to encourage habit formation.
  • Caregiver notifications for additional support.

3️⃣ How might we reduce health risks?

  • Drug interaction alerts to prevent harmful combinations.
  • Health insights & reports to track adherence patterns.

Task flow - Key steps

Task Flow is a step-by-step sequence that outlines how a user completes a specific task within an app or system. In a medication reminder task flow, users select a medication, set a reminder, receive notifications, and manage reminders ( edit, delete, turn off).

Accessibility requirements ♿📲

A well-designed medication reminder app ensures accessibility for users of all abilities, especially seniors and those with cognitive or physical impairments, following WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines).

🔹 Clear & Readable UI 🧐📱

  • Large, high-contrast text for easy readability
  • Simple, intuitive navigation with minimal steps

🔹 Voice & Audio Assistance 🎙️🔊

  • Voice commands for setting reminders (Alexa/Siri integration) (non-mvp)
  • Audio alerts with customizable tones

🔹 Haptic & Visual Feedback

  • Vibration notifications for those with hearing impairments
  • Flashing screen alerts for added visibility

🔹 Multimodal Input

  • Touch-friendly buttons with large tap areas
  • Option for keyboard or voice input

🔹 Cognitive Support

  • Easy-to-understand icons and labels
  • Step-by-step guidance for setup

We conducted a usability test to evaluate the effectiveness of the Medication Reminder feature in the Patient Access app. The goal was to ensure users can easily find, understand, and set up medication reminders while identifying areas for improvement.

Key Objectives & Findings

✅ Task Success & Navigation Clarity

  • 85% of users successfully found and set up medication reminders.
  • Navigation was rated 4.2/5, with most users finding the process intuitive.
  • Some users expected to access reminders from their Medical Record instead of the Main Menu.

✅ Understanding Consent & Naming Clarity

  • 60% of users understood the consent information.
  • The term "Medication Reminders" was the most preferred naming choice (75%), as it was straightforward.
  • Some confusion on "Regular Intervals" vs. "Daily", indicating a need for clearer descriptions.

✅ UI Affordance & Interaction

  • Clickable reminder cards were well-received, but some users struggled to differentiate active vs. disabled reminders.
  • Users preferred a visual cue (color or icon change) for disabled reminders.

✅ Likelihood to Use & Future Enhancements

  • 70% of users indicated they would use medication reminders regularly.
  • Users requested additional notification options, such as mobile alerts for medication refills.
  • 50% of users wanted adherence tracking to monitor their medication intake.

Post MVP Hurdels and Gamification 🎯

After the successful launch of the MVP (basic medication reminder system), user feedback highlighted the need for better engagement, motivation, and deeper adherence insights. To address this, streak-based gamification and adherence reports were introduced.

Challenges Identified Post-MVP 🔥

  1. Drop in Long-Term Engagement – Users initially responded well to reminders but lost motivation over time.
  1. Lack of Self-Tracking – Users wanted to see their progress beyond daily reminders.
  1. Limited Caregiver Involvement – Caregivers needed better ways to monitor adherence trends.
